<div class="case-intro">
  <p class="lead">Dimers, Cipher Sports' flagship app and website, helps US sports fans find betting opportunities. NBA Research Lab (the internal name) is a new way to explore Dimers' NBA player projections, included with Dimers Pro: projections, sportsbook lines and recent games in one place, so a bettor can answer one question fast: over or under?</p>
  <p class="lead">I designed it as a product design intern, two days a week, from discovery and whiteboard sketches to an interactive prototype that became the team's spec, through to QA on TestFlight.</p>

<Section num="01" title="Challenge" id="challenge">
  <h3>"Do I go over or under?"</h3>
  <p>Dimers' models already projected every NBA player prop, but to research one, bettors left for tools like props.cash or ESPN, and the session left with them.</p>
  <p>The brief was discovery-led: one sport, one section of the app, and a settled direction before the NBA season tipped off in late October.</p>
  <Callout kind="goal">
    <p>Let Pro subscribers research a prop and make their own call without leaving Dimers.</p>
  </Callout>
  <p><b>Principles</b>: mobile-first, clarity over density, progressive depth, and research, not noise.</p>
</Section>

<Section num="02" title="Ideation" id="ideation">
  <h3>Starting on the whiteboard</h3>
  <p>The core question: how to show the data so it's both informative and easy to read, with recent games as context for the projection, not the headline.</p>
  <p>I started by listing what a bettor needs before placing a prop: availability, the Dimers projection, bookmaker odds, historical stats, the game log and the bet itself. Then I sorted them into an order: projections and bookmakers side by side, then trend stats, then the game log.</p>
  <div class="media-row">
    <Media src={sketchNeeds} alt="Whiteboard: six circles listing what a bettor needs: availability, Dimers projection, bookmaker odds, historical stats, game log and the bet" />
    <Media src={sketchFlow} alt="Whiteboard: player page order, from projections and bookmakers to trend stats and the game log" />
  </div>
  <h4>The list row</h4>
  <p>The projections list had to show a whole slate at a glance. I sketched rows that put a player's last games next to the line, with the gap between our projection and the line on the right, so the list itself starts the answer.</p>
  <div class="media-row">
    <Media src={sketchRows} alt={'Whiteboard: list row explorations with icon, name, recent games against the line and the projection gap, under the question "Do I go over or under?"'} />
    <Media src={sketchList} alt="Whiteboard: early projections list and player row ideas with recent games and the line" />
  </div>
  <h4>The player page</h4>
  <p>Iterating on the player page, I removed the separate Dimers projection box and folded it into the chart as tonight's bar, and moved availability and the game log to their own Games tab so the Stats tab stays about the line.</p>
  <Media src={sketchPlayer} alt="Whiteboard: three iterations of the player page layout, ending with Stats and Games tabs" />
  <Callout kind="decision">
    <p>Put the line at the centre of the chart, and let the user switch it between the market consensus, any sportsbook, or a line they drag themselves.</p>
  </Callout>
</Section>

<Section num="03" title="Solution" id="solution">
  <h3>From a slate to a single prop</h3>
  <h4>1. Projections list</h4>
  <p>Every NBA player expected on tonight's slate, across points, rebounds, assists and more, in one sortable list: recent games as dots above or below the line, the line, our projection and the gap between them.</p>
  <h4>2. Player page</h4>
  <p>A stat strip (a dot marks stats where the model found value), line chips for the consensus and each sportsbook, and a hit-rate chart that shows how often the player cleared the line in his last 5, 10 or 20 games, head to head, home or away. Tonight's projection sits at the end as a dashed yellow bar.</p>
  <Media src={screens} alt="The projections list, a player page with a best bet selected, and the player page scrolled with its header collapsed" />
  <h4>3. The line decides everything</h4>
  <p>Picking a chip moves the line, and the bars, percentages and labels all follow. When the selected sportsbook line is our best bet, the line, its tag and the bet card turn green. Other books stay blue.</p>
  <Media src={lines} alt="Player page with the consensus line in blue, next to the same page with the best-bet sportsbook line in green" />
</Section>

<Section num="04" title="Interaction Details" id="details">
  <h3>Small things that make the chart readable</h3>
  <ul>
    <li><b>Numbers never sit on the line</b>: a bar's value and gap label move just above or below the line when it runs through them.</li>
    <li><b>Tooltips from the bars only</b>: tap a bar for that game, then slide a finger to move across games. Tapping empty space does nothing.</li>
    <li><b>A header that follows the scroll</b>: inspired by Sofascore, the headshot and name glide into the top bar as you scroll, and the matchup becomes a small line above the name.</li>
  </ul>
  <Media video="media/nba/player-scroll.mp4" frame="phone" alt="Player page: the header collapses into the top bar while scrolling, then a finger scrubs across the hit-rate bars" />
  <Callout kind="fix">
    <p>On the first TestFlight build the page flickered near the bottom: collapsing the header resized the scroll area, which pushed the page back to the top and expanded it again. Keeping the header at one fixed height, with the player row scrolling away as part of the page, broke the loop.</p>
  </Callout>
</Section>

<Section num="05" title="Share Image" id="share">
  <h3>Sharing what you see</h3>
  <p>Bettors already share picks on X and Instagram, so the player page can turn the current view into a branded image. The image always matches the state on screen: the consensus line, a sportsbook's line with its price, the best bet in green, or a line the user dragged.</p>
  <Media src={share} alt="Two share images for Anthony Edwards: a best bet, Under 7.5 assists at DraftKings, and a DraftKings points line" />
  <p>It comes in two sizes: a 4:5 post for X, Instagram feed, DMs and messages, and a 9:16 story. Next, taking a normal screenshot on the player page will offer the share image straight away.</p>
</Section>

<Section num="06" title="Shipping It" id="shipping">
  <h3>A prototype as the spec</h3>
  <p>Instead of static screens, I built an interactive prototype of every stage of the rollout. It became the team's single source of truth: stage-by-stage scope, a tracking plan, and the data each feature needs from the API.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><b>Staged rollout</b>: the list and player page first, then best bets and the player rail, then the scrolling header, dragging your own line and sharing.</li>
    <li><b>Working with engineering</b>: I wrote the Jira tickets and acceptance details for each change, and answered developers' edge cases, like what happens when a best bet's price moves.</li>
    <li><b>QA on TestFlight</b>: I tested each build on device, recorded issues and posted fixes back to the tickets with expected behaviour.</li>
  </ul>
</Section>

<Section num="08" title="Learnings" id="learnings">
  <ul>
    <li><b>Design the states, not the screen</b>: most of the work was in the edge cases: no line posted, a price that moved, a long player name, a stat with tiny numbers.</li>
    <li><b>A working prototype speeds up decisions</b>: being able to tap through the real behaviour settled debates that screenshots never would have.</li>
    <li><b>Shipping is part of design</b>: the details people notice, like a flicker or a cut-off letter, only show up on a real device, so QA belongs in the design process.</li>
  </ul>
</Section>
